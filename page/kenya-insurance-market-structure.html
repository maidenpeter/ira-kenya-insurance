Method

How the numbers are built

Written premium is gross direct premium plus inward reinsurance for each insurer at the fourth quarter, year to date, from IRA's quarterly returns as extracted and checked in ira-kenya-data. Insurers only, micro insurers included. 2017 is missing because IRA has no year-end 2017 workbook online.

The Herfindahl-Hirschman index is the sum of each insurer's squared percentage share, from 0 to 10,000. The US Department of Justice and Federal Trade Commission treat 1,000 to 1,800 as moderately concentrated and over 1,800 as highly concentrated. One divided by the index gives the equivalent number of equal-sized insurers.

  • Fragmentation cost takes the five largest insurers' management expenses over gross premium in each class as the benchmark, and compares every other insurer's actual expenses with what it would spend at those rates on its own mix. Insurers leaner than the benchmark reduce the total. It is a measure of the size of the gap, not a claim that every insurer could reach that cost: part of it may be the price of serving smaller clients.
  • Growth runs from 2018 to 2025 for insurers with at least KSh 0.5bn of premium in 2018 that still report in 2025. Growth and share are nominal.
  • Share shifts are pro forma for three mergers: Saham into MUA, and Allianz Kenya and Sanlam General into what is now Sanlam Allianz General.
  • Companies are joined across renames with the sourced crosswalk in ira-kenya-data. Entry and exit sources are in reference/entries_exits.csv.

Source: Insurance Regulatory Authority, Kenya, quarterly industry statistics, full years 2016 to 2025 (unaudited returns). Concentration thresholds: US Department of Justice, Herfindahl-Hirschman Index, under the 2023 Merger Guidelines. Entries and exits: Capital FM, IRA on X, Allianz, The Star, Business Daily. Everything here is public data.

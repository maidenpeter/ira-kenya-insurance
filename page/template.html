Method

How the numbers are built

Every figure comes from IRA's quarterly industry statistics: each insurer's class tables at the fourth quarter, year to date, as extracted and checked in ira-kenya-data. Insurers only, micro insurers included, which is how IRA counts the market. The class totals match IRA's own PDF industry releases for 2020 to 2024.

The premium change comes from the loss ratio method of ratemaking, with commissions as a variable expense and management expenses as fixed, all on earned premium:

change = (loss ratio + expense ratio) ÷ (1 − commission ratio − target margin) − 1

Werner and Modlin, Basic Ratemaking, Casualty Actuarial Society, 2016.

  • The rate change is an indication of the size of the gap, not a filed rate. It has no loss trend, no development beyond what insurers booked, and no charge for capital, so the true need is higher.
  • The investment income version credits each class with the whole market's investment income as a share of earned premium. Much of that income is earned on shareholders' capital, so this version flatters the classes.
  • Net commissions go negative in heavily reinsured classes such as fire industrial and engineering, where reinsurers' commissions exceed what insurers pay out.
  • A low loss ratio is a warning, not proof. It can come from a good book, heavy reinsurance or thin reserves. The test compares ten insurers with twenty-eight, a small sample.
  • 2016 is left out because IRA's 2016 class tables have no earned premium or expense split. IRA has no year-end 2017 workbook online.

Source: Insurance Regulatory Authority, Kenya, quarterly industry statistics, full years 2018 to 2025 (unaudited returns). Regulator actions: Capital FM (Resolution, April 2022; Xplico and Invesco, December 2023), IRA on X (Trident and Corporate, March 2026). Pricing context: in September 2024 the Court of Appeal held that IRA may regulate premium rates but struck down its 2009 motor minimum rate guidelines as disproportionate (EY tax alert). Everything here is public data.
